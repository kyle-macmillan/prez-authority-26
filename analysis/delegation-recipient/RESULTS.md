# Results: delegation recipient

The alternative recipient-focused analysis does **not yet validate an exact 96%
corpus-wide figure**. It does show that agency-only statutory grants are rare among
the provisions resolved so far, while shared President–agency grants are substantively
important and should not be folded into a binary presidential category.

- 5,405 pinpoint authority-unit occurrences were extracted from formal vesting clauses.
- 2,834 (52.4%) presently have source-supported recipient classifications.
- Among 2,756 resolved affirmative power conferrals, 2,413 (87.6%) are President-only,
  25 (0.9%) are agency/officer-only, and 318 (11.5%) confer power on both.
- If shared grants count as containing presidential power, the resolved share is 99.1%.
- If the question is exclusively President versus exclusively agency/officer, the
  presidential share is 99.0%.
- Seventy-eight resolved citations confer no affirmative power on either recipient.

The appropriate current statement is therefore:

> In the source-supported portion of the census, nearly all statutory provisions that
> confer power on an exclusive executive recipient confer it on the President. About
> one in nine resolved power-conferring citation occurrences, however, assigns relevant
> authority to both the President and an agency or officer. Because 47.6% of pinpoint
> occurrences still require historical or uncodified-source resolution, these figures
> are coverage-qualified and are not a final census estimate.

The remaining work is concentrated in repealed or renumbered provisions, ambiguous Act
references such as “section 7 of the Act,” and clauses listed in
`outputs/unmatched_specific_signals.csv` that the conservative parser did not normalize.
